Evolution of Typological Thinking

Throughout history, thinkers have tried to categorize human behavior. Ancient models focused on bodily fluids, while early 20th-century psychodynamic models emphasized psychic energy orientation and cognitive functions. Understanding these models allows us to appreciate psychological history while recognizing their scientific limitations.

1. Hippocrates & Galen: The Four Humors (c. 400 BCE - 170 CE)

Ancient Greek physician Hippocrates hypothesized that bodily health and human temperament depended on the balance of four bodily fluids (humors). Roman physician Galen later expanded this into four distinct psychological temperaments:

Blood
Sanguine

Air Element • Warm & Moist

Enthusiastic, cheerful, optimistic, social, but prone to impulsivity and lack of focus.

Yellow Bile
Choleric

Fire Element • Warm & Dry

Ambitious, decisive, assertive, natural leader, but easily irritated, aggressive, or quick-tempered.

Black Bile
Melancholic

Earth Element • Cold & Dry

Analytical, thoughtful, detail-oriented, loyal, but susceptible to sadness, anxiety, and perfectionability.

Phlegm
Phlegmatic

Water Element • Cold & Moist

Calm, peaceful, reliable, empathetic, but can exhibit passive resistance, apathy, or slowness to act.

2. Carl Jung: Attitudes & Psychological Functions (1921)

Carl Gustav Jung revolutionized typology by shifting from physical fluids to **Psychic Energy (Libido)** orientation and 4 fundamental mental functions.

Two Fundamental Attitudes
Extraversion (E):

Libido/energy is directed outward toward objective external objects, social encounters, and environmental events.

Introversion (I):

Libido/energy is directed inward toward subjective internal thoughts, reflections, and mental constructs.

Four Psychological Functions
1. Thinking (Rational): Evaluates truth through logic & systemic analysis.
2. Feeling (Rational): Evaluates worth based on ethical values & harmony.
3. Sensing (Irrational): Perceives immediate facts via the 5 physical senses.
4. Intuition (Irrational): Perceives hidden patterns, future possibilities & hunches.

Critical Inquiry: Why Typologies are NOT Diagnostic Tools

In modern scientific psychology (e.g., DSM-5 or ICD-11), historical typologies like the Four Humors or Myers-Briggs (MBTI derived from Jung) are **never used as clinical diagnostic instruments**.

1. Binary Oversimplification Categorizing people into strict "either/or" boxes ignores continuous dimensional spectrums (e.g., most people are Ambiverts).
2. Low Psychometric Validity Historical typologies lack empirical test-retest reliability and predictive power for clinical psychopathology.
3. The Barnum Effect Vague statements make descriptions seem personally accurate to anyone, creating an illusion of validity.
